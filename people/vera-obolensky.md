# Vera Obolensky

**Vera**—**Nemtchinova** before the world insisted on **Princess Obolensky**—fled the **1917** storm south to **Tehran**, where she ran what functioned as a **forty-room hotel** on **[Mahmoudieh](../topics/mahmoudieh-house.md)**: European beds, Russian tongues, **[Étienne Stump](etienne-stump.md)**’s land beneath her lease and later his name beside hers in marriage. Her son **Georges**, born **1920** in the same city, would write the memoir that saves the guesthouse from anecdote.

## Life

- **Born** c. 1895, Russian Empire (age 79 at death in Nov 1974; NYT death notice). Maiden name recorded as **Nemtchinova** (per [Ancestry record](https://www.ancestry.com/genealogy/records/georges-obolensky-24-1tby3yk) for son Georges). **Not** the ballerina [Vera Nemtchinova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vera_Nemtchinova) (1899–1984), a different person.
- **Marriage to Prince Yuri Obolensky** — before or during WWI. The Obolenskys were a prominent Russian princely family. The NYT death notice describes Yuri as "**a relative of Tolstoy**," connecting the Obolensky line to the Tolstoy family.
- **Flight to Persia** — after the 1917 Revolution, Vera and Yuri fled south through the Caucasus to Iran. By **1920** they were living in Tehran.
- **1920** — son **Georges (Yuri) Obolensky** born in Tehran.
- **c. 1925** — Prince Yuri dies (malaria). Vera remains in Tehran as a widow with young **Georges**.
- **Mid-1920s–1950s** — manages the **Mahmoudieh guesthouse** on [Dr. Stump's estate](../topics/mahmoudieh-house.md). See [The guesthouse](#the-mahmoudieh-guesthouse) below.
- **Marriage to Dr. Étienne Stump** — in Tehran after the guesthouse was established; before Georges' 1954 visit he learns they had married while he was in France ([*Forever Russian* ch. 9](../sources/corpus/forever-russian-stump-extract/transcription.md)).
- **c. 1951** — after Stump's death, Vera leaves Iran for **Frankfurt**, Germany, to join her son and daughter-in-law.
- **1950s–1960s** — settles in **New Jersey**, USA.
- **6 November 1974** — dies in New Jersey. Death notice published in the *New York Times*, **14 November 1974**.

## The Mahmoudieh guesthouse

After **Prince Yuri** died (**c. 1925**, malaria), **[Étienne Stump](etienne-stump.md)** — who already owned the **Mahmoudieh** hillside estate and villa ([Hamshahri 2022](../sources/corpus/hamshahri-mahmoudieh-stump/translation.en.md)) — asked Vera to run his **lodging business**. Their son **Georges** described it as a **“bed-and-breakfast-type hotel with 40 plus single cabins”** on the compound between **Tajrish** and **Evin** ([*Forever Russian*, ch. 1](../sources/corpus/forever-russian-stump-extract/transcription.md)).

**Who came.** Not tourists in the modern sense, but **European businessmen** dispatched by their **companies** for **short postings** in Tehran — long enough to need beds, meals, and help with Iranian officialdom, not long enough for embassies to house them. Vera's **English, French, and German** made her the natural front desk; Georges calls her an **astute businesswoman** who had **no problems attracting** that trade.

**What she offered.** A European-run **forty-room** operation (cabins plus main house) on garden land that already signalled wealth — Stump's **antiques and carpets** ([Sayyah, via Beytoote §4](../sources/corpus/beytoote-stump-dentist-atabak-ahmadshah/translation.en.md)). Vera's edge was **practical**: she **helped guests deal with the Persian bureaucracy**, backed by an **Armenian assistant** fluent in **Farsi and Russian** for the slow work of permits and papers. That combination — Swiss property owner with court connections, Russian princess with company contacts — made the guesthouse a hub for **inter-war foreign business in Tehran** before the Hilton era.

**Division of labour.** Stump kept his **dental practice** downtown (**Lalezar** / **Ala al-Dowleh** — always “packed,” per MP **Kamareh'i**, 1917–1920). He was **owner** of the B&B; Vera **managed and expanded** it. Georges grew up on site (**Mlle. Tomassin's French school**), seeing little of his mother except across a crowded guesthouse. Stump and Vera's partnership became a **marriage** sometime before Georges' **1954** return; the son then understood the **loneliness** that had driven his widowed mother into Stump's orbit ([ch. 9](../sources/corpus/forever-russian-stump-extract/transcription.md)).

**Henriette Bottin** and the Stump children had largely left Iran **c. 1920**; the guesthouse belongs to the **Vera** chapter of Mahmoudieh, not the first marriage. [Robert](robert-marc-murard-stump.md) was born Tehran **1910** but raised in Europe.

## Context: Russian émigrés in Iran

Vera was part of the first wave of Russian refugees (White Army officers and nobility) who fled to Iran after the 1917 Revolution and Civil War. Hundreds of thousands of Russians passed through; some settled permanently. The community built the **St. Nicholas Orthodox Church** in Tehran (opened 1945). Academic research by **Marcus James** (ANU) documents a second wave of refugees in the 1930s and their eventual emigration in the 1950s — see ["Exploring the Russian Refugee Diaspora in Iran, 1930–1955"](https://www.peripheralhistories.co.uk/post/exploring-the-russian-refugee-diaspora-in-iran-1930-1955) and ["Mechanics Fleeing Communism"](https://cais.cass.anu.edu.au/research/publications/mechanics-fleeing-communism-russian-refugees-iran-and-their-resettlement) (open-access paper). Also: [Kaveh Farrokh / RT report on St. Nicholas Church](https://www.kavehfarrokh.com/uncategorized/a-view-of-russian-emigres-to-iran/) and Wikipedia [Russians in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Iran).
