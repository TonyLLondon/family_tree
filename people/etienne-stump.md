# Étienne Stump

In Persian letters and memoirs he is the court dentist with a surname that never quite settles—اشتومپ، اشتمپ، استامپ—while in English reference books he is often called Austrian or German. The record here is simpler: he was Swiss, born in Tallinn when the city was still the Russian Empire’s Reval, and he spent nearly half a century in Tehran shaping how the capital thought about modern teeth.

That reputation was earned in public rooms and private houses alike. Visitors remember a busy practice toward the end of Lalezar, porcelain sets priced in tomans, and a garden estate in Mahmoudieh that later fed into one of north Tehran’s most recognizable neighbourhoods. Simin Daneshvar’s novel *Savūshūn* even turns his name into a proverb: a proper smile meant a proper set of teeth from Dr Eshtamp—evidence, if any were needed, that he had crossed from foreign specialist into folk memory ([*Savushun* excerpt](../sources/corpus/savushun-daneshvar-schtomp-false-teeth-ghanoonparvar-pp366-367/transcription.en.md); Internet Archive `savushunnovelabo00dani`).

The [Europe-only line from Thurgau to Tallinn](../stories/stump-thurgau-tallinn-baltic-line.md) explains how a Swiss father and a Baltic German mother could raise a son beside the Gulf of Finland. What came next was stranger still: training in Chicago, a bruising brush with an Illinois licensing board — the State Board refused his license **30 April 1902** (German-American Dental College diploma); Judge Chetlain dismissed mandamus that summer, with the court caption spelling **Etienne Stump** ([*Dental Digest*, Aug 1902](../sources/corpus/1902-dental-digest-etienne-stump-mandamus/transcription.md); [Tribune/*Dental Register* reprint](../sources/corpus/dental-register-stump-mandamus-1902/transcription.en.md)) — hire in New York as dentist to the grand vizier on an American tour, and then decades beside Ahmad Shah’s court and the classrooms of Dār al-Funūn, where chroniclers credit him among the first to teach dentistry in a systematic way.

He was arrested once in the Great War’s paranoia, released as a neutral national, and outlived his first marriage and the Qajar world he had first known. From the **mid-1920s** his second act at **[Mahmoudieh](../topics/mahmoudieh-house.md)** was as much innkeeper as dentist: a **forty-cabin guesthouse** on the hillside estate between **Tajrish** and **Evin**, run by **[Vera Obolensky](vera-obolensky.md)** for European company men on short Tehran postings — with Stump supplying the land, the introductions, and the Persian connections while Vera supplied languages, nerve, and an Armenian fixer for the bureaucracy.

Through it all he accumulated land, beer-making interests north of the city, and the ambiguous blessing of wealth: one Majles debate even recycled the tale that only the Shah and Stump owned motor cars in 1912—likely embroidery, but telling.

## Mahmoudieh guesthouse

By the time **[Vera Obolensky](vera-obolensky.md)** took over operations, Stump already owned a large slice of the old **Qajar garden-palace** tract — bought c. **1918** from **Mohammad Hossein Amin al-Zarb**, with a villa, farming, antiques, and carpets ([Hamshahri 2022](../sources/corpus/hamshahri-mahmoudieh-stump/translation.en.md); [Mustowfi vol. 1, p. 51](../sources/corpus/mustowfi-mahmoudieh-dr-stemp-dli-508037/translation.en.md); [Sayyah via Beytoote §4](../sources/corpus/beytoote-stump-dentist-atabak-ahmadshah/translation.en.md)). His **clinic** stayed on **Lalezar** / **Ala al-Dowleh**, packed with patients including MPs ([Kamareh'i diary, 1917–1920](../sources/corpus/beytoote-stump-dentist-atabak-ahmadshah/translation.en.md) §5). **Mahmoudieh** was the country house and, under Vera, a parallel business.

**Prince Yuri Obolensky** died of **malaria c. 1925**. Soon after, Vera was asked to manage what her son **Georges** remembered as a **“bed-and-breakfast-type hotel with 40 plus single cabins”** on Stump’s property ([*Forever Russian* — ch. 1](../sources/corpus/forever-russian-stump-extract/transcription.md)). Stump was **owner**; Vera **managed and expanded** it. Georges, then not quite five, grew up in the compound while his mother worked sixteen-hour days.

The clientele was deliberately commercial. Vera spoke **English, French, and German** — the reason Stump hired her, Georges thought — and had **no trouble attracting guests sent from Europe** by their firms for **short-term assignments** to meet Iranian management. She was, in his phrase, an **astute businesswoman** for someone of princely Russian background: she helped those men **navigate the Persian bureaucracy**, with an **Armenian assistant** who was fluent in **Farsi and Russian** for the tedious official chores. For inter-war Tehran, that made the Mahmoudieh compound something close to a **foreign-run business hotel** before such a category existed in guidebooks.

Georges paints the household from a child’s eye: **Mlle. Tomassin’s French grammar school**, a lonely boy, mother always at the guesthouse, **Dr. Stump** the Swiss dentist who owned the cabins and, in time, Vera’s partner and husband. The son did not yet understand that their friendship was also a romance born of **widowhood and exile** — Vera explained that loneliness only when Georges returned in **1954**, after Stump’s **1951** death ([*Forever Russian* — ch. 9](../sources/corpus/forever-russian-stump-extract/transcription.md)).

Stump’s first wife **[Henriette Bottin](henriette-bottin.md)** and their children ([Robert](robert-marc-murard-stump.md), [Theodore](theodore-stump.md), [Irene](irene-stump.md), [Jacques](jacques-stump.md), [Jean](jean-stump.md)) moved to **Geneva** c. **1920**, leaving Persia for Europe; the Mahmoudieh guesthouse belongs to the **Vera decades**, not the Henriette household. How exactly Henriette’s marriage ended is still unresolved in the vault.

## Family

His parents were [Marc Francois Stump](marc-francois-stump.md) and [Olga Caroline Erbe](olga-caroline-erbe.md). He married [Henriette Bottin Burgess](henriette-bottin.md)—not [Fanny Burgess](fanny-burgess.md)—and later [Vera Obolensky](vera-obolensky.md), who ran the **Mahmoudieh guesthouse** on his land before their marriage. Children include [Robert Marc Murard Stump](robert-marc-murard-stump.md), [Theodore Stump](theodore-stump.md), [Irene Stump](irene-stump.md) (“Loulou”), [Jacques Stump](jacques-stump.md), and [Jean Stump](jean-stump.md).

Persian and Anglophone secondary works disagree on arrival year and on his age at death; parish Tallinn gives a firm March birth in 1880, while syndicated obituary arithmetic sometimes implies an earlier decade. The [synthetic Persian article on Beytoote](https://www.beytoote.com/art/negah-gozashte/dentist-atabak2-ahmadshah.html), widely mirrored, is a convenient hub for the Atabak–Ahmad Shah thread ([extract](../sources/corpus/beytoote-stump-dentist-atabak-ahmadshah/extracted.web.md); [annotated English](../sources/corpus/beytoote-stump-dentist-atabak-ahmadshah/translation.en.md)).
