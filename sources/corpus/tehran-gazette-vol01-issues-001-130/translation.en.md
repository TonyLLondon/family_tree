## NLIR preface — staff list (PDF p. 4, ~1994)

The reprint editors warn that **gazette staff rotated constantly** under Naser al-Din Shah and that naming everyone from start to finish would take years of study. The **best-known** hands include:

1. **Hajj Mirza Jabbar** (Tadhkira-chi; father of Mirza Javad Khan Saʿd al-Dawla) — **manager** of the newspaper.  
2. **Berjīs Ṣāḥeb** (Edward Burgess) — **translator** of the newspaper.  
3. **Mirza ʿAbd Allāh** — **Persian writer** for the newspaper.

This is **modern scholarly framing**, not an 1850s issue masthead — but it is the clearest **Persian-language naming of Burgess inside the NLIR volume**.

## Issue 1 (7 February 1851)

**Head title:** *Gazette of news of the Abode of the Caliphate, Tehran* (early form of the state paper).

The opening frames the publication as ordered by **Nasir al-Din Shah** so subjects and merchants may be educated and informed of domestic and foreign news — the standard Qajar official idiom for Amir Kabir’s state gazette project, without naming ministers or European staff.

**Section:** News of the capital (Tehran).

## Issue 2 (14 February 1851)

**Title:** *Rūznāma-ye Vaqāyeʿ-e Ettefāqiye* (“Journal of happening events”) — the name used from issue 2 onward (per Persian Wikipedia and the masthead).

**Content type:** Domestic “Guarded Domains” news with a **Tehran** dateline — weather, prices, local incidents — typical of the inner columns Edward later supervised together with translated **foreign news** sections on other pages.

## Foreign news — England (PDF p. 19)

Mixed page: **provincial Iran** (Mazandaran, Isfahan, Gilan) then a dedicated **England** section — **London** news, **East India Company** vessels, **Bombay/Calcutta** sailings. This matches Edward’s role translating European papers for the court ([crosswalk](edward-gazette-crosswalk.md)).
