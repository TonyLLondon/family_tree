# Rufus Anderson — *History of the Missions* (vol. I, 1872)

American Board foreign secretary Rufus Anderson compiled this institutional history from mission archives, the *Missionary Herald*, and correspondence. **Volume I** includes the long **Nestorian** (Oroomiah / Lake Urmia) narrative used here.

The same compilation is often reprinted under the shorter shelf title ***American Foreign Missions to the Oriental Churches*** (volume numbering varies by edition).

**Popular digest:** Robert Young's anthology *Light in Lands of Darkness* prints a condensed parallel account (Nestorian / patriarch / Dawood Khan / English Consul / royal death); see [Young corpus reference](../robert-young-light-lands-darkness-nestorian-1848/reference.md).

## Transcription: Dawood Khan governor of the Nestorians (1845)

**Context in Anderson:** Nestorian mission, chapter treating **1841–1848**; immediately before Anderson’s account of the **1846** revivals on the plain of Oroomiah.

**Source:** verbatim from the vault PDF extract (`extracted.pdf.md`, block following `<!-- page 118 -->` in the ingest) and cross-checked against [Gutenberg `pg18930.html`](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/18930/pg18930.html) (search `In May, 1845`).

```
In May, 1845, the Shah, at the instance of the English and Russian
Ambassadors, appointed Dawood Khan, of Tabriz, an Armenian from
Georgia and an officer of the army, Governor of the Nestorians. The
object was to protect the Christians from the oppressions they had
long suffered from the Moslem nobles residing in the district.
```

**Genealogical / political reading:** independent confirmation that **Dawood/Daoud Khan of Tabriz** was understood as **Armenian from Georgia** and an **army officer**, given a **Qajar civil title over Nestorians** after joint **British and Russian** diplomatic pressure, with stated purpose **protection of Christians** against **local Muslim notables** (not against the American mission).

## Transcription: Christian Governor and civil protector (later 1840s)

**Context:** Patriarch Mar Shimun, Jesuit activity, and conflict with the American mission; Anderson explains why Mar Shimun and the Jesuits wanted Dawood Khan removed.

**Source:** same volume in `extracted.pdf.md` (block after `<!-- page 123 -->` in the ingest); align wording in Gutenberg HTML with search `civil protector`.

```
For a time the Patriarch and the Jesuits, both aiming at the
overthrow of the mission, were in practical combination. As a
necessary means to this end, both wished to expel from office Dawood
Khan, the Christian Governor and civil protector of the Nestorians
of that province; and the Mohammedan nobles were in sympathy with
them in this, as that dignitary stood in the way of their exactions.
```

This ties **Daoud Khan’s** office to **mission–patriarch–Jesuit–consular** politics on the **Urmia plain** and to **Mr. Stevens**, English Consul at Tabriz, in the paragraphs that follow in Anderson.

## Transcription: 1848 outcome (patriarch, Jesuits, Dawood Khan, Stevens)

**Context in Anderson:** Chapter XIX — chronicle of **1848**: Mar Shimun's open opposition, rebuff by the **mullah**, patriarch–**Jesuit** alignment against **Dawood Khan**, unsolicited aid from **Mr. Stevens** (English Consul), Nestorian **representation** to Tehran, **Mohammad Shah's** death, removal of **Yahyah Khan** as governor of Oroomiah, bonds on Mar Shimon's **coadjutors**.

**Source:** verbatim from the vault corpus extract [`history-of-the-missions-of-the-american-board-of-651655d656/extracted.pdf.md`](../history-of-the-missions-of-the-american-board-of-651655d656/extracted.pdf.md) (`<!-- page 134 -->` through first paragraphs on page 135); punctuation modernised only where the extract preserved chapter headings mid-flow.

```
But this political alliance, though at first promising success both to the Patriarch and the Jesuits, in the end led to the signal overthrow of both. It was stated to the mission by Mr. Stevens, the English Consul, as a well ascertained fact, that Mar Shimon had united his interests with the French Jesuits, and that they had strong hope of making use of him to cast their net over his people. Up to this time, the mission had not applied to any European functionary for interference in their troubles with Mar Shimon. Nor did they now; but Mr. Stevens, hearing of his persecuting course, took up the matter of his own accord, gave the information as above stated, and befriended the missionaries in various ways. The Patriarch having declared, that he had the countenance and support of the Russian Consul, that official wrote sharply rebuked him for so doing. The four bishops of Oroomiah and nearly all the priests and deacons, with many of the leading Nestorians in the province, now united in a representation to the Persian Government, highly commending the character, objects, and labors of the mission. It is recorded, that the converted Nestorians also, with scarce an exception, stood firmly by the mission, in the face of trials and reproaches; and the same was true of many who made no pretensions to piety.

News of the death of the King arrived at Oroomiah on the 14th of September. He was succeeded by his eldest son, a young man of twenty years, who for the last year had been Prince Governor of Azerbijan. In Persia, the death of the King interrupts for a time the regular transaction of public business.

An immediate effect of the news was to displace the Governor of Oroomiah, Yahyah Khan, with whom Mar Shimon had been forming an alliance, to strengthen him in his persecutions.

Through the friendly, but unsolicited agency of the English Consul, five of the most prominent of Mar Shimon's coadjutors were put under heavy bonds in no way to aid or abet him again in similar proceedings. Should they violate their written engagements to the authorities, they would expose themselves to severe corporal punishment and heavy fines.
```

**Genealogical reading:** mission narrative confirms **Dawood Khan** still framed as **Christian governor / civil protector** while **Mar Shimun** and **Jesuits** sought his removal; failure of that alliance involved **English consular** initiative at **Tabriz**, provincial Nestorian petition to **Tehran**, and **dynastic transition** (**Mohammad Shah** d. September 1848; **Naser al-Din** as heir apparent ruling Azerbaijan) displacing **Yahyah Khan** — independent of family memoir but same political universe as the Saginian–Cormick–mission network.

## Summary

Institutional history of the **American Board** missions in the Eastern churches. **Volume I** includes the **Nestorian (Lake Urmia / Oroomiah)** narrative: schools, revivals, patriarchal politics, and **Qajar** responses.

## Passages used in this project

### May 1845 — Dawood Khan / Daoud Khan Saginian

Anderson records the shah’s appointment of **Dawood Khan of Tabriz**, described as **an Armenian from Georgia** and an **army officer**, as **Governor of the Nestorians**, at the request of the **English and Russian Ambassadors**, in order to **protect Christians** from **Muslim nobles’** oppressions. Full verbatim block: [reference](reference.md#transcription-dawood-khan-governor-of-the-nestorians-1845).

### Later 1840s — civil protector vs patriarch / Jesuits / nobles

Anderson calls him the **Christian Governor and civil protector of the Nestorians of that province** and states that the **patriarch**, **Jesuits**, and **Muslim nobles** wished to **expel him** because he blocked **their exactions**. [reference](reference.md#transcription-christian-governor-and-civil-protector-later-1840s).

### 1848 — alliance fails; Nestorian petition; Stevens; royal death; Yahyah Khan displaced

After Mar Shimun's rebuff by the **mullah**, Anderson narrates the failure of the patriarch–**Jesuit** move against **Dawood Khan**, **Mr. Stevens**' unsolicited defence of the mission, a Nestorian **representation** to Tehran, **Mohammad Shah's** death (**14 September** at Oroomiah tidings), and bonds on Mar Shimon's **coadjutors**. Full verbatim block: [reference](reference.md#transcription-1848-outcome-patriarch-jesuits-dawood-khan-stevens).
