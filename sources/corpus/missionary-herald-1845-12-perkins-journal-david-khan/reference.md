# *Missionary Herald* (Dec 1845) — David Khan, Governor of the Nestorians

**Note on voice:** The Herald attributes the block to **Perkins’s journal**. The visit to David Khan is narrated in the first-person plural (**“we”**); the singing exercise on **5 July** appears as **“Mr. Perkins held …”** — likely editorial third person in the printed Herald, not evidence of a different author for the David Khan visit.

## Context in the journal

The passage follows Perkins’s account of **summary justice in Tabriz** (butchers punished for withholding meat from the market). It precedes notes on a **female seminary** singing exercise (**5 July 1845**). The David Khan visit belongs to the same **June–July 1845** mission tour when Perkins was reporting **religious interest at Geog Tapa** and wider **Oroomiah** progress — after the **May 1845** shah’s appointment recorded in [Rufus Anderson](../rufus-anderson-1872-history-missions-oriental-churches/reference.md#transcription-dawood-khan-governor-of-the-nestorians-1845).

## Transcription — visit to David Khan (verbatim sense; punctuation modernised)

**Source:** Internet Archive `sim_missionary-herald_1845-12_41_12_djvu.txt`, cross-checked against vault facsimile [`daoud-khan-missionary-herald-1845-shepherd-quote.png`](../../../media/docs/daoud-khan-missionary-herald-1845-shepherd-quote.png). Minor OCR repairs (`perform`, `circumstances`, `expresses`, `trust, however`, `right`).

```
Such is the summary method of doing things in Persia.

We called also on David Khan, the Governor of the Nestorians. He appears
to be a good man, for a native, and very well qualified for his office.
He remarked that he had a very hard task to perform, in encountering the
opposition of the Mohammedans to his administration, which is
undoubtedly true; but sustained, as he is, by the Russian and English
Embassies, there is much reason to hope that he will be successful.
"The Nestorians," he said, "are like sheep surrounded by wolves, and I
am their shepherd;" which, so far as their temporal circumstances are
concerned, well expresses the state of the case. We trust, however, that
the hand of the Lord has much more to do in this matter, than either he,
or those who sent him hither, have in mind; and we fondly cherish the
belief that the great and good Shepherd has important designs of mercy
towards this scattered and peeled flock, in this beneficent arrangement.
It is happy for us, however, as missionaries of the cross, that we have
as little occasion, as we have right or inclination, to involve ourselves
in any such matters.
```

**Following sentence in the same journal (date anchor):** *Mr. Perkins held a singing exercise, July 5, with some of the girls connected with the female seminary.*

## Genealogical reading

- **Independent missionary witness** (not family memoir) that **David Khan** was understood locally as **Governor of the Nestorians** in **summer 1845**, soon after the shah’s appointment.
- **Direct quotation** — the only vault capture of Daoud Khan’s **own words** on the Nestorian brief: pastoral metaphor (**sheep / wolves / shepherd**), **Muslim opposition** to his administration, and reliance on **Russian and English** diplomatic backing.
- Perkins’s gloss distinguishes **temporal protection** (David Khan’s office) from **spiritual hope** (“the great and good Shepherd”) — typical American Board framing; still preserves the governor’s self-description intact.
- Aligns with [Anderson (1872)](../rufus-anderson-1872-history-missions-oriental-churches/reference.md) on appointment timing and purpose; adds **character judgment** (“good man, for a native”) and **quoted policy rationale** Anderson does not print.
