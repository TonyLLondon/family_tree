# Footnote (125) — English

> (125) It is rather curious that our *Mémoire* says absolutely nothing about the function of “Sarparast,” which had been created by Mohammed Shah in May 1845, at the joint request of the English and Russian ambassadors. It was a special governor for the Christians of the region. The first person appointed was **Daoud (or David) Khan Saginian (or Séguinoff)** of Tabriz, an **Armenian from Georgia**, an officer in the Persian army and favourable to the Protestants (Anderson, vol. 1, p. 325; Perkins, *Missionary Herald*, 45 (1849), p. 26). His daughter **Anna (1815 – 8 January 1892)** married **Edward Burgess** in the autumn of 1851, whom we discuss below, note 142. One can still see her tomb, as well as that of their son **John**, who died at the age of twenty-one months on **16 November 1853**, in the Armenian church of **Saint Thaddeus–Bartholomew** in Tehran. ## Why this closes “Was Anna a Saginian?”

The footnote names **Saginian** and **Séguinoff** as **the same man** — Anna’s **father** — not two competing identities. Anna’s NYPL **Seguinoff** is that surname as the English interviewer heard it.
