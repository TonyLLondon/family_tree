## Left page

**WHO WAS MY FATHER**

My father, Hovsep Sarginian, born in Tabriz in 1899, was the youngest son of Colonel Solomon Khan Sarginian and Nunya Ter-Ohanian Sarginian. Hovsep had an older brother named Alec and two orders sisters, named Sophia and Astghik. He had one older half-brother, Zora (Sohrab) Sarginian, from Solomon's fist wife who had died after childbirth; and a stepbrother, Bagrat Ter-Ohanian, who came in as the son from Nunya's first deceased husband.

Nunya was from the Bzhishkian family and had two sisters, Zabel (Isabel), and Manya, and two brothers Ruben and Vahan. Beyond that I do not know much about their ancestry. Those brothers and sisters were referred to as Zabel Khala, Manya Khala, Ruben Dayi, and Vahan Dayi. Khala (mother's sister) is Persian for aunt in its Turkic form, the Persian form is Khale, and Dayi (mother's brother) is also a Persian word for uncle.

The family tree of Solomon Khan is an interesting one. It dates to Ashkanian and Parthian kingdoms of the Persian Empire. But, instead of going into that narration, I will go directly into the line of heritage of my father. His father was Colonel Gorgin Khan Saginashvili, and his father was Brigadier General David Khan Saginashvili.

Who were they and why did they change their names is rather interesting. On the 24th of October 1813, a treaty was signed between the Imperial Russia and the Government of Iran, known as the Treaty of Gulistan, a small village in Azerbaijan. According to that treaty, the States in the Caucasus, namely Armenia, Georgia, Azerbaijan and Daghestan, were given to Russia. That treaty put an end to the Iran/Russia war in the Caucasus. Heracles II, the last Bagratouni King of Georgia would not accept to go under Russian rule. He decided to move to Iran and live in

## Right page

Iran as a refugee from Georgia. Three officers accompanied him. Those officers were, Colonel David Khan Saginashvlli, and his two sons, Captain Gorgin Khan Saginashvili and Captain Zali Khan Saginashvili. Crown Prince Alexei was with them.

For those who have not seen Georgia and mainland Iran, it will be hard to understand what happened when they reached Tehran, the capital of Iran. Just imagine, if you please, a mountainous Garden of Eden that was Georgia, and a mountainous semi-desert wasteland that was central Iran. It did not take too long for the young Prince Alexei to decide that he could not stay. They all agreed that Captain Zali Khan Saginashvlli would accompany Prince Alexei back to Georgia. King Heracles II would take asylum in Iran and reside in a section of the capital known as the Armenian Quarter. Captain Gorgin Khan Saginachvilli was promoted to the rank of Colonel and assigned to the Garrison of Azerbaijan, headquartered in Tabriz, as the commander of the cavalry. Colonel David Khan Saginashvilli was promoted to the rank of Brigadier General and assigned to the Military training school in Isfahan (Sepahan), as the commander and instructor of the artillery.

Captain Zali Khan Saginashvilli accompanied Prince Alexei back to Georgia. General David Khan Saginashvilli went to Isfahan, and Colonel Gorgin Khan Saginashvilli went to Tabriz. General David participated in several battles with rebellious tribes and served in Isfahan until retirement. After retirement he moved to Tabriz, he died and was buried in a family plot in Tabriz. Why a family plot? It was a customary practice among nobilities of the time to have their own family burial grounds. It is known as Mary, Mother of God, cemetery. Besides Saginians, only a few of high-ranking Christian nobilities are buried there. When I went to Georgia, I met very many people with the surname Saginashvili. I was wondering what kind of people they were. I learned that there, in Georgia also, Saginashvilis had their own cemetery also.
