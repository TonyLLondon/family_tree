# Interview with Anna [Mrs. Edward] Burgess

Manuscript dated **C. Belmont Villas, Leicester, March 1880**. Printed in Schwartz's edition as the **Appendix** (NYPL Bulletin pp. 458–460). Authoritative full-volume transcript: [burgess-persian-letters-full-volume/transcription.md](../burgess-persian-letters-full-volume/transcription.md).

## Extract (opening — family structure)

> My ancestors, on the sides of both parents, were Armenians, and I think for some time have lived in Tabriz. My father, Daoud Khan Seguinoff, was always in the military service of the Shah of Persia. My dear mother died some time before him. My uncles were men in high position. We were six in family—two brothers and four sisters. Both of my brothers were merchants in Tabreez; both are now dead. I am the eldest of the sisters. My next sister is the widow of Aga Pedros an Armenian merchant. She lives in Tehran, and has three sons. My youngest sister, Tamar, is the widow of Dr. Cormick.

> We had known Charles and Edward Burgess a long time. … Edward Burgess asked for me of my father. I loved him even before we were married. Then we were married at my father's house…

## Extract (Edward's death and cemetery)

> When Fanny was three days past one year old, he died. We had been married just five years.

> He was buried in the little lovely green Armenian cemetery just outside the walls of Tabriz, close by the side of my uncle. … My dear father's grave is four or five English yards from it; it is among the graves of all my relations. And old Mr. & Mrs. Cormick and the Europeans are all buried there. The trees are very beautiful.

## Note on spelling

**Seguinoff** is the interviewer's romanisation of Anna's Armenian patronymic for "son/daughter of David" — not a separate surname. Elsewhere the family fixed **Saginian** (see [Tamar Hovnatanian portrait](../../corpus/connectionsbmc-saginian-interview/), Cormick chart **DAOUDIAN**).
