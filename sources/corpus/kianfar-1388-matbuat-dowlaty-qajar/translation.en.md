# Kianfar 1388 — English (Edward Burgess passages only)

From Jamshid Kianfar, *Matbūʿāt-e dowlati-ye Īrān dar ʿaṣr-e Qājār*, *Payām-e Baharestān*, spring 1388, pp. 277–279.

## Staff, translator, copyist, print shop

The management of the newspaper was in the hands of **Ḥājī Mīrzā Jabbar** the *tadhkira-chī* — later known as **Nāẓim al-Mamālek** — and the **translator of the newspaper was the Englishman Edward Burgess**. The newspaper’s **editor/copyist** was named **Mīrzā ʿAbd Allāh**, who wrote in a fine nastaʿlīq hand; it was printed at **Ḥājī ʿAbd al-Muḥammad**’s press at the **Doulāb** gate, and from issue **99** onward it was printed at the **Dār al-Khilāfah** press in Tehran.

*(Same section: the paper was published by order of Mīrzā Taqī Khān Amīr Kabīr under his supervision; he sometimes wrote domestic news himself.)*

## Distribution and pay

Each week, on **Fridays**, the newspaper was sent to the provinces by **courier/post**; the fee for the **next issue** was collected **and paid to Edward Burgess**.

## Foreign news

The paper’s material was mostly: “news of the capital,” “news of the other protected provinces,” **foreign affairs**, miscellaneous items, and **announcements**.

**Foreign news** included reports from **England, Russia, France, Austria, Italy, Portugal, the Ottoman lands, India**, and other European countries. **This news was translated by Edward Burgess** from **European newspapers, especially English ones**, and **sometimes by others** from **Turkish newspapers printed in Istanbul**. The translated items passed through **one or two people** — in today’s terms, **edited** — and in the language of that time were **polished into literary Persian**. The translated news was **brief**, and for people of that day it was **very useful** for learning about conditions abroad.

Persian forms in the article: **ادوارد برجیس انگلیسی**; **«برجیس صاحب»** on the editing step.
